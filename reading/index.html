Reading

Books, papers, and essays that shaped how I think about healthcare, technology, and what comes next. This list grows.

Healthcare, and what AI is doing to it

The books closest to the argument I am making.

Deep Medicine
Eric Topol

The book that framed the question for a lot of us: can AI give clinicians back the time to be human with patients. Written before generative AI, which makes its predictions worth revisiting.

A Giant Leap
Robert Wachter

Wachter wrote the definitive account of healthcare's last technology disappointment, so his cautious optimism about this one carries weight. The clearest current picture of AI actually entering hospitals.

AI for Good
Josh Tyrangiel

Reported from inside institutions trying to make AI work, including the parts that fail. A corrective to both the hype and the doom.

Understanding the technology

What it is, what it can do, and what it costs.

Co-Intelligence
Ethan Mollick

The most practical book on working with AI rather than being replaced by it. Mollick's jagged frontier is the framing I use most.

The Coming Wave
Mustafa Suleyman

The containment problem, argued by someone who helped build the thing. Worth reading because it takes the risks seriously.

Prediction Machines
Ajay Agrawal, Joshua Gans, Avi Goldfarb

The economics: AI makes prediction cheap, and everything downstream of prediction gets reorganized. A clean way to think about which parts of healthcare change first.

The Worlds I See
Fei-Fei Li

A memoir and a history of computer vision at once. The human story behind the technical one.

AI Engineering
Chip Huyen

The practitioner's book. What building on top of these models actually involves.

Thinking, deciding, and leading

How ideas arrive, and how decisions get made under uncertainty.

Where Good Ideas Come From
Steven Johnson

The adjacent possible. Innovation as recombination in connected environments rather than lone genius.

Farsighted
Steven Johnson

How to make hard decisions well. For anyone choosing between futures rather than optimizing the present.

The AI-Driven Leader
Geoff Woods

Practical framing for leaders trying to use AI for thinking rather than just for output.

What it means to be human

The questions underneath all of this.

Klara and the Sun
Kazuo Ishiguro

A novel about an artificial friend who observes human love more carefully than the humans do. Worth reading before designing anything that claims to be a companion.

A World Appears
Michael Pollan

A journey into consciousness. If we are going to build things that converse with us, it is worth understanding what we mean by a mind.

Papers and essays

Primary sources worth reading in full rather than in summary.

Machines of Loving Grace
Dario Amodei

The most specific argument anyone has made about what AI could actually do for human health, including a section on compressing decades of biomedical progress into years. Worth reading alongside the risk literature rather than instead of it.

Managed Agents
Anthropic Engineering

How agent systems get built and bounded in practice. If you want to understand what an orchestration layer actually has to do, start here rather than with the vision pieces.

The Adolescence of Technology
Dario Amodei

Anthropic's CEO on where the technology is in its own development, and what that implies for how we should be handling it.

Claude's Constitution
Anthropic

The published values framework for a frontier model. Useful reading for anyone thinking about what it means to give a system boundaries, which is most of the work in building one.

LeJEPA: Provable and Scalable Self-Supervised Learning Without the Heuristics
Randall Balestriero, Yann LeCun

Technical, and worth the effort. LeCun's line of work on models that learn structure from observation rather than from labels, which is a different bet than the language models get most of the attention.

The 8 Types of AI That Matter to Business Leaders
Chris Hood

A plain-language map of model types for people making decisions about them. Useful for anyone tired of every tool being called AI.
