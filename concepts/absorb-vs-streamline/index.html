Absorb vs. Streamline

Definition

Streamlining makes the existing channel faster. Absorbing takes the work off the channel entirely. Every previous wave of health technology streamlined the aperture; agentic systems are the first that can absorb what passes through it.

Portals, telehealth, reminder systems, and automated outreach all made the visit-centered model marginally more efficient. None of them could take work away from the care team, because absorbing coordination requires something that can hold context over time, converse, act across systems, and persist between encounters.

That distinction matters more than any capability benchmark. A tool that streamlines returns minutes to the clinician, which the system immediately reallocates to more throughput. A system that absorbs changes what has to pass through the aperture at all.

The design consequence is not substitution. It is division of labor: software takes the coordination, so the scarce human attention goes to judgment, uncertainty, and trust — the things that actually require a person.
